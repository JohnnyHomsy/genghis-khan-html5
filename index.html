Name and Title

There is not universal system for Mongolian. Many modern spelling and pronunciation of Mongolian names vary greatly. The honorific most commonly rendered as "Genghis" ultimately derives from the Mongolian ᠴᠢᠩᠭᠢᠰ, which may be romanised as Činggis.

Early Life

The year of Genghis' birthday is also debated among historians. However, many believe it is either 1155 or 1167. Genghis was born into the Borjigin clan of the Mongol tribe[f] to Yesügei, a chieftain who claimed descent from the legendary warlord Bodonchar Munkhag. Several legends surround Genghis's birth. The most prominent is that he was born clutching a blood clot in his hand, a motif in Asian folklore indicating the child would be a warrior.

Rise to Power

At fifteen, Temüjin married Börte and used a sable cloak from her family as a gift to win the protection of Toghrul, a powerful Kerait ruler, which let him start attracting his own followers. Not long after, Merkit raiders kidnapped Börte; Temüjin got help from Toghrul and his sworn friend Jamukha to rescue her, and she soon gave birth to Jochi, whose parentage was questioned for the rest of his life. She and Temüjin went on to have three more sons and four more daughters. Temüjin and Jamukha camped together for about eighteen months before splitting up, and though most of the established tribal leaders sided with Jamukha, dozens of chiefs and many commoners followed Temüjin, drawn by his reputation for fairness and generosity. His men declared him khan, which pushed the rivalry with Jamukha into open war; Temüjin was beaten at Dalan Baljut around 1187, and historians think he likely spent much of the next decade serving the Jin dynasty in northern China before returning to power.

Quick facts

  • Born: c. 1162, Khentii Mountains
  • Died: August 1227
  • Reign: 1206–1227
